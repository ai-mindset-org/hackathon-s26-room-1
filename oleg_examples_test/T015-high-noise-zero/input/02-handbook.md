# ECHO-ZERO Cooperative operations handbook
Edition: 1 February 2028
Document family: ECHO-ZERO/Handbook

## Reading regional records
New York registers normally show dates as month/day/year, such as 04/05/2028 for April 5.
Brussels and Rotterdam registers normally show dates as day/month/year, such as 04/05/2028 for 4 May.
A copied date keeps the interpretation of its named source register.
US financial displays may show $1,234.50, while EU displays may show EUR 1.234,50.
The punctuation is part of the source convention and does not indicate a revised amount.

## Suggested meeting record structure
The standard page begins with topic, context, alternatives, decision, and decision date.
A facilitator can add owner and target date after the group reaches a decision.
Example prompt: Could ECHO-ZERO/Sample publish a two-page guide for new visitors?
Example conditional: If the sponsor approves ECHO-ZERO/Sample, the group would select a format.
The example name is reserved for training pages and is never used for an operational project.

## Calendar housekeeping
Room holds begin as tentative calendar entries with a requester and a local time zone.
A declined hold stays visible in calendar history so invitees can understand the empty period.
A canceled series can retain individual occurrence dates in an export made before cleanup.
Replacement meetings receive new invitations rather than edited copies of declined holds.
Regional holidays appear beside room entries to explain building-access patterns.

## Status language
A digest may describe an option as proposed, compared, paused, retired, rejected, or completed.
Authors should preserve those words when quoting an older decision.
Questions belong under discussion notes until the source group records an answer.
Ideas from an open chat can be copied to a later agenda without changing their status.
Historical completion dates remain useful for audit and trend comparisons.

## Automated summaries
The summary service groups repeated nouns and dates that occur near each other in a source feed.
Quoted mail, calendar metadata, and reaction counts can therefore appear in one paragraph.
Readers open the original thread when the summary wording differs from the source chronology.
System timestamps use UTC even when the summarized material uses a regional convention.
A confidence percentage describes text grouping, not approval by a person or committee.
